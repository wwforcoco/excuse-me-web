About This Tool

This calculator estimates your due date and how far along you are using the standard clinical method — 280 days from the first day of your last period, or 266 days from a known conception or IVF transfer date. It's an estimate for planning purposes, not a medical diagnosis.

Note: This tool provides a general estimate only. Your healthcare provider may adjust your due date based on an ultrasound or your specific medical history — always follow their guidance over any online calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a pregnancy due date calculated from the last period?

The standard method adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period, assuming a typical 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14.

Why does pregnancy counting start before conception actually happens?

Because the exact conception date is rarely known, gestational age is counted from the first day of the last period instead, which puts you about two weeks "pregnant" before conception technically occurs.

How accurate is a due date estimate?

It's a guideline, not a guarantee. Only a small percentage of babies arrive on their exact estimated due date, and a full-term birth anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks is considered normal.

How is the due date calculated from a known conception date?

Add 266 days to the conception date. That's 280 days minus the roughly two weeks between the last period and ovulation, since conception happens after the cycle has already started.

Does an irregular cycle length change the due date?

Yes. A longer or shorter average cycle shifts when ovulation likely occurred, so the estimate adjusts the standard 280-day calculation by the difference between your cycle length and the 28-day average.

Is an ultrasound more accurate than a last-period calculation?

Early ultrasounds, especially between 6 and 8 weeks, are generally considered the most accurate dating method, since they measure the embryo directly rather than relying on memory of the last period.

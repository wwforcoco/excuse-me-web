§ 01 — Philosophy
Manifesto

Architecture is the patient arrangement of light, material, and silence. We are not in the business of spectacle. We are in the business of making places that remain — that age with grace, that ask little of their inhabitants and give much in return.

A door should open the way you expect it to. A wall should hold the warmth of the afternoon. A window should frame the sky as if it were the first time you had seen it. These are small ambitions. They are also the whole of the work.

We believe in fewer materials, used better — in concrete that remembers the formwork, in timber that darkens with the years, in stone that carries the mark of the hand. We believe a building is finished not when the contractor leaves, but when the first occupant sets down their coat.

Everything else is style. We are not interested in style.

— The Studio New York, 2026
i.

Site First

Every site has a memory. We listen before we draw, reading the land, the wind, the path of the sun across a single year.

ii.

Material Honesty

Concrete, timber, stone, plaster. Materials used for what they are, never imitating something they are not.

iii.

Quiet Detail

The detail that disappears into use. A threshold you cross without noticing — until you notice how right it feels.

iv.

Enduring Form

A building should outlast its architects and its first program. We design for the second life, and the fifth.

§ 03 Process

Four movements, repeated until the building sings.

Our process is deliberately slow at the start and fast at the end. We would rather spend three months understanding a site than three weeks undoing a mistake.

01/04

Listen

Site visits, conversations, archival research. We map the existing before we propose the new — the wind, the neighbours, the history, the budget.

02/04

Design

Sketches become models become drawings. We test in physical material — clay, cardboard, plaster — long before we render in pixels.

03/04

Build

We remain on site. Weekly visits, weekly minutes, a single point of contact. The drawing is a contract; the site is the proof.

04/04

Inhabit

A year after handover we return. We measure how the building has been used, what has shifted, what has held. The lessons feed the next project.

§ 05 The Studio

Four principals, one quiet conversation about what a building should be.

FORMA was founded in 2008. We are sixteen people across three studios, working on no more than nine projects at a time.

Elena Marchetti

Founding Partner

Trained in Milan and Zurich. Elena leads the residential practice and the studio's material research. Teaches at the Politecnico.

Kenji Watanabe

Partner, Kyoto

Joined FORMA in 2012. Kenji leads the cultural portfolio and our Kyoto studio. Formerly of SANAA, specialist in timber and joinery.

Marcus Hollander

Partner, Berlin

About This Tool

This calculator estimates your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiplies it by your activity level to estimate Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), the calories you burn maintaining your current weight. Pick a goal to see a suggested daily calorie target.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy your body burns at complete rest just to keep your organs running. TDEE adds movement, exercise, and digestion, giving you the actual number of calories you burn across a full day.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

A common approach is to eat around 500 calories a day below your TDEE, which works out to roughly one pound of fat loss per week, since a pound of body fat is estimated at about 3,500 calories.

Is it safe to cut calories more aggressively than that?

General health guidance advises against losing more than about two pounds a week or going far below 1,200 calories a day without medical supervision, since a bigger deficit risks muscle loss and a slower metabolism.

Which activity level should I pick if I'm not sure?

When in doubt, choose the lower option. People who assume they're moderately active are often closer to lightly active once actual daily movement, not just scheduled workouts, is accounted for.

Why does this calculator ask for sex and age?

Both affect resting metabolic rate. Men generally carry more lean muscle mass, which burns more calories at rest, and metabolic rate tends to decline gradually with age, so both factors shift the formula.

How accurate is a calorie calculator like this one?

It's a solid starting estimate, not a lab measurement. Two people with identical stats can burn a few hundred calories a day apart, so treat the result as a starting point and adjust based on your actual weight trend over a few weeks.
